# 2. Company Story

Sangam Tours started in Nagpur in 1979, when organised group travel in Maharashtra was still a new idea. Nearly five decades later, the business still runs on the same premise it opened with: a traveller shouldn't have to manage the logistics of their own holiday.

Over that time, Sangam Tours has run group departures across some of India's more demanding routes — the high passes of Leh-Ladakh, the monsoon coast of Konkan, the desert circuits of Rajasthan — as well as international tours through Southeast Asia, Bhutan, and Nepal. Each of these carries its own operational load: permits, altitude, seasonal weather, multi-state transport. Sangam Tours absorbs that load internally so the traveller doesn't have to think about it.

Three things have stayed constant since 1979: a Tour Manager present on every departure, home-style meals on domestic tours, and a clear breakdown of what's included and excluded in every package — something the company did before it became a standard industry expectation.

It's a Nagpur-based, family-run operator that has kept the same travellers coming back across two generations, and is now reaching a new generation through Instagram and Facebook.

# 4. Mission

Sangam Tours fulfils this vision by designing fixed-departure group tours that remove every point of friction from travel planning. This means:

- Pre-negotiated accommodation, transport, and meals bundled into transparent, all-inclusive pricing
- A trained Tour Manager present on every departure to handle logistics, coordination, and on-ground problem-solving
- Domestic itineraries built around home-style meals, comfortable group transport, and realistic daily pacing
- International itineraries that bundle flights, entry tickets, and local transport so travellers are not left to coordinate fragmented bookings
- A booking and enquiry process — via call, WhatsApp, or online form — that is fast, direct, and does not require the traveller to chase for information

Every package, every itinerary, and every piece of website content should be evaluated against one question: does this make the traveller's decision easier and their trip smoother?

# 6. Brand Personality

Rather than assigning numeric scores that imply a measurement nobody actually took, personality traits are ranked by how much weight they should carry in design and copy decisions:

**Most defining traits — Trustworthy, Reliable.** Both are earned by the 47-year track record and transparent pricing, not by tone of voice, so these should show up as *evidence* (dates, testimonials, inclusions) rather than adjectives in the copy.

**Core to voice — Family-Oriented, Approachable, Friendly, Professional.** These should shape how the site talks to visitors day to day: warm, direct, structured, never stiff.

**Present but secondary — Confident, Modern.** The brand can carry these without needing to assert them; they come through in execution quality (a well-built site, a clear enquiry flow) rather than in stated claims.

**Deliberately restrained — Premium, Adventurous.** Premium: the brand is comfortable and well-managed, not luxury — avoid positioning language that implies exclusivity or high-end pricing. Adventurous: present in itineraries like Leh-Ladakh, but not the dominant note against the gentler pilgrimage and coastal packages that make up much of the catalogue.

# 9. Customer Problems

| Customer Concern | How Sangam Tours Solves It |
|---|---|
| "Will the price change after I book?" | Every package explicitly lists Included/Not Included, and Terms & Conditions clarify when prices are subject to change (airfare, GST, fuel costs) — set as an upfront expectation, not a surprise. |
| "Who will help us if something goes wrong on the trip?" | A dedicated Tour Manager accompanies every group departure. |
| "Is this trip suitable for my parents / children?" | Segment-specific package framing (pilgrimage, coastal, hill-station) and clear pacing information addressed on each package page. |
| "How do I actually reach someone before booking?" | Three direct CTAs on every package page: Book Now, WhatsApp Us, Call Now — no forced account creation. |
| "Is this a real, established company?" | "Since 1979" positioning, physical Nagpur and Akola offices, and customer testimonials establish legitimacy. |
| "What happens if I need to cancel?" | Cancellation & Refund Policy is clearly linked and referenced in the Terms & Conditions. |
| "Will food and stay be comfortable, not just cheap?" | Home-made food (domestic), accommodation, and daily essentials (like water bottles) are stated inclusions, not vague promises. |
| "Is this trip physically demanding or risky (e.g., high altitude)?" | High-altitude itineraries explicitly mention oxygen cylinder provisioning and required permits. |

# 10. Customer Emotions

**Before booking:** Hesitation and comparison-shopping. Travellers are weighing whether this operator can be trusted with their family's time and money. The dominant emotion is a need for reassurance — proof of legitimacy, clarity on cost, and an easy way to ask questions without commitment.

**During travel:** Relief and enjoyment. Once on tour, the traveller should feel the absence of logistical stress — someone else is handling transport, stay, and coordination. This is where trust, once earned, gets reinforced for repeat business and referrals.

**After returning:** Satisfaction and social validation. Travellers want to share their experience — photos, stories, recommendations to friends and family. This is the moment testimonials and referrals are generated.

**How the website should reinforce these emotions:**
- Before booking: reduce hesitation through visible trust signals (since 1979, testimonials, transparent pricing) placed early on every page, not buried at the bottom.
- During travel (represented through content): show real group photography and itinerary structure that proves organisation and care, not just destination beauty shots.
- After returning: make it easy to find and read testimonials, and give the website a tone that travellers would be proud to have recommended.

# 11. Brand Voice

| Element | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Tone | Warm, direct, and reassuring — like a knowledgeable family friend who has organised this trip many times before. Never salesy, never stiff. |
| Sentence Length | Short to medium. Avoid long, clause-heavy sentences, especially on package and CTA copy. |
| Vocabulary | Everyday, familiar English with occasional Indian-English travel terms (e.g., "sightseeing," "tour manager") that the audience already recognises. Avoid Western travel-industry jargon ("bespoke," "curated getaway," "wanderlust"). |

| Words to Avoid | luxury-only language ("opulent," "exclusive elite"), backpacker slang ("epic," "insta-worthy," "vibe"), vague marketing filler ("unforgettable," "magical" used repeatedly), corporate jargon ("synergy," "solutions") |

# 15. Design Principles

1. **Emotion Before Information** — Open every package page with a strong visual and a clear emotional promise before presenting the logistics.
2. **Trust-First Layout** — Trust signals (since 1979, Tour Manager, testimonials) should never be more than one scroll away from any CTA.
3. **Large, Real Destination Imagery** — Photography drives conviction in travel; use large, high-quality visuals of actual destinations covered in packages, not generic stock travel imagery.
4. **Three Clear CTAs, Always Visible** — Book Now, WhatsApp Us, and Call Now must remain accessible without requiring the visitor to scroll back up.
5. **Minimal Clutter** — Package pages carry a lot of structured information (dates, pricing, inclusions); layout must organise this cleanly rather than compress it.
6. **Premium, Breathable Spacing** — Generous white space signals a well-run, professional operator, not a discount travel listing site.
7. **Mobile-First** — The overwhelming majority of traffic arrives from Instagram and Facebook ads on mobile devices; every layout decision starts at mobile width.
8. **Fast Loading** — Ad-driven traffic has low patience; optimise images and assets aggressively so package pages load quickly on mid-range mobile networks.
9. **Accessibility for All Ages** — Given the senior citizen segment, ensure sufficient font size, colour contrast, and simple navigation patterns throughout.
10. **Consistent Visual System** — Use the defined brand colours (Navy Blue #09235C, Royal Blue #2E3190, Accent Green #91C44D, White) and typography (Bebas Neue for logo, Poppins for body) consistently across every page.
11. **Transparent Structure Over Persuasion Tactics** — Present pricing and inclusions plainly; avoid manipulative urgency tactics ("Only 2 seats left!") that would undercut the brand's trust-first positioning.
12. **Effortless Enquiry Flow** — The enquiry form should feel short and low-effort even though it captures multiple fields; group fields logically and avoid unnecessary steps.

# 16. What We Are NOT

| We Are Not | Why |
|---|---|
| A budget ticket-booking website | Sangam Tours sells fully managed group experiences with a Tour Manager and structured inclusions — not standalone transport or stay bookings. |
| A backpacking or independent-travel blog | The audience is families, seniors, and organised groups seeking managed travel, not solo explorers seeking DIY advice. |
| A luxury-only travel company | Packages are positioned as comfortable and well-managed, not exclusive or premium-priced; overreaching into luxury positioning would misrepresent both pricing and audience. |
| A government tourism portal | The site must feel like an active, responsive business — warm and commercial in tone, not bureaucratic or informational-only. |
| A visa consultancy | Sangam Tours explicitly states it is not responsible for visa outcomes; the website must not imply visa-handling services. |
| An OTA marketplace (like a generic booking aggregator) | Sangam Tours sells its own curated fixed-departure packages, not a searchable marketplace of third-party listings. |

# 17. AI Design Rules

These rules apply to all AI coding agents (including Cursor) generating code, components, or layouts for this project. Where a rule here echoes a point in §15 (Design Principles) — mobile-first, accessibility, performance — treat §15 as the *why* (the design philosophy) and this section as the *how* (the enforceable build rule). They are one requirement stated twice for two audiences, not two separate, weaker ones.

1. Maintain consistent spacing values across all components; do not introduce arbitrary padding/margin values outside the defined design system scale.
2. Never invent new button styles, colours, or CTA treatments — use only the three defined CTA types (Book Now, WhatsApp Us, Call Now) and their established visual treatment.
3. Follow the design system strictly: Navy Blue (#09235C), Royal Blue (#2E3190), Accent Green (#91C44D), and White as the only brand colours; Bebas Neue for logo/display use, Poppins for all other text.
4. Prefer reusable components (e.g., a single PackageCard component reused across the homepage, listings, and search) over one-off, page-specific markup.
5. Avoid unnecessary animations; motion should be purposeful (e.g., subtle transitions on CTA hover) and never delay access to information or CTAs.
6. Maintain visual consistency across all package pages — every package should follow the identical layout structure regardless of destination.
7. Build mobile-first; design and test at mobile breakpoints before scaling up to tablet and desktop.
8. Treat accessibility as a first-class requirement: sufficient contrast ratios, readable font sizes, and clear navigation — particularly given the senior citizen audience segment.
9. Treat performance as a first-class requirement: compress and lazy-load imagery, minimise unnecessary JavaScript, and keep package pages fast on mobile networks.
10. Every package page must include, without exception: pricing, dates, destinations covered, Included/Not Included list, and all three CTAs.
11. The enquiry form must always capture: Name, Mobile Number, Email, City, Package Interested In, Number of Travellers, Preferred Travel Date, and Message — and store each submission with a timestamp.
12. Do not fabricate content, testimonials, pricing, or package details that are not present in the source requirements — flag missing information (e.g., Akola office address, Google Maps link) rather than inventing it.
